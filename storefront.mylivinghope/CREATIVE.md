# Creative Direction: My Living Hope Storefront

## The Problem

The current site is competent but forgettable. It looks like "another Tailwind site" — clean, functional, and completely interchangeable with ten thousand other sites selling candles, journals, or essential oils. Council audit (10 design-focused members) was unanimous: the bones are good but the soul is missing.

## Creative Principles

### 1. Lead with the wound, not the pitch

The hero currently opens with a resolution ("Go Deeper With Jesus") before establishing the problem. The real hook is buried in section 3: "Helping You Pray Without Shame or Confusion."

**The shift:** Open with the emotional reality — the awkward silence, the not-knowing-what-to-say, the feeling like a fraud. Make the visitor feel *seen* before selling anything. The site should whisper "you don't have to know the words yet" before it says "here's a product."

### 2. The card IS the brand

Prayer Portals are physical cards. The card shape (specific aspect ratio, rounded corners) should become THE recurring visual motif across the entire site — not just in the hero, but for testimonials, steps, info blocks, decorative elements. Cards that tilt, fan, stack, peek from edges. One shape, used with discipline, is worth more than a dozen Tailwind effects used once each.

Currently the site uses 5 different decorative languages (floating cards, offset borders, gradient circles, glassmorphism, blurred blobs). Kill four. Keep the card.

### 3. Break the metronomic rhythm

Every section currently has identical padding (py-20/py-28), identical containers (max-w-1400), and identical headers (section-tag + H2 + subtitle). Six times in a row. Prayer isn't metronomic — it has long pauses, then a flood of words, then silence.

**The shift:** Varied section heights. Some sections dramatically tall with breathing room, others compact and intimate. At least one full-bleed moment that breaks the 1400px container. A Scripture-only section that's nothing but a single line on a colored field — a pause between movements.

### 4. Show, don't describe

The site describes the product six different ways but never *shows* it. The hero, product showcase, homepage about section, and about page all render the same placeholder rectangle. One well-photographed card fanned on a table replaces every placeholder.

Until real photos arrive: design for no photos. Use the card-as-object motif (illustrated/stylized cards with real content from the actual product) instead of generic gradient boxes with book icons.

### 5. Typography as emotion

Currently every heading uses the same clamp formula. There's no tension. The Libre Baskerville italic on "With Jesus" and "Pray" is the right instinct — lean into it harder.

**The shift:** Some type enormous and commanding. Some whispered and intimate. Pull quotes that interrupt the flow. A heading that spans the full viewport width. The type scale should have the same dynamic range as a prayer — from "help" to "HALLELUJAH."

### 6. Texture over polish

The site is too clean for something born from watching young people struggle. Frank Ocean: "A prayer card should feel like opening a letter someone wrote you at 2am. This feels like opening a brochure."

**The shift:** Subtle texture — paper grain, watercolor washes, hand-drawn elements. Not grunge, not messy — but the warmth of something handmade. The gap between "professional wellness brand" and "thing someone made because they cared" is where this site needs to live.

## Homepage Restructure

Current: Hero → Product Showcase → About → How It Works → Testimonials → CTA

Proposed narrative arc:

1. **The Wound** — Hero that makes you feel seen ("You want to pray but don't know where to start")
2. **The Origin** — Jesse's story, why these exist (moved UP from section 3)
3. **The Product** — Show the cards, how they work, what's on them (merge current sections 2+4)
4. **The Proof** — Real testimonials with real names and specific moments
5. **The Invitation** — CTA that echoes the opening ("You don't need the right words. Just begin.")

Five sections, not six. Each one earns the next.

## Reference Touchstones

- **Kinfolk Magazine** — editorial whitespace, typography-forward, intentional rhythm
- **Headspace** — emotional product, warm illustration, simple but distinctive
- **Field Notes** — physical product pride, texture, the object as hero
- **Apple product pages** — let one product fill the entire screen, scroll-driven storytelling

## What's Off-Limits

- No more gradient placeholder rectangles with book icons
- No more identical section headers (tag + H2 + subtitle)
- No SaaS copywriting ("Transform Your Prayer Life!", "Join thousands")
- No glassmorphism (it says "2021 dashboard," not "prayer")
- No feature checklists with green checkmarks
